MeeSeva Dashboard

Internal system for a local MeeSeva center In Development

Overview

MeeSeva Dashboard is an internal service-center management system designed around the day-to-day workflow of a local MeeSeva center — customer management, service information, applications, queue and token operations, staff permissions, collections and reporting. It's designed from operational requirements upward, with attention to application/token state separation, financial privacy, and scope control.

It is an internal tool for the center owner and staff. It is not an official government MeeSeva replacement and does not integrate with government systems.

The Problem

Running a service center involves many small operational tasks that are easy to lose track of without a system:

  • Managing customer information
  • Tracking applications
  • Handling the queue
  • Managing service information
  • Tracking collections internally
  • Organizing daily operations

The Approach

The system is structured around the center's actual areas of work:

Customer Management Service Management Applications Queue / Tokens Collections Staff / Permissions Reports Settings

In daily use, that structure plays out as:

Customer search / create→ Select service→ Create application→ Generate token→ Process through queue→ Update application status→ Complete / submit→ Track internal collection

Every step is performed by the owner or staff. This is not an online customer self-service portal. Built with attention to requirements, workflow design, full-stack development, database, and authentication.

My Role

I planned and designed the dashboard around how the center actually works, defining its workflow, data, and access rules, and I am building it. It has not been deployed or adopted in real-world use.

Core Features

Customer Management

Search, create, and manage customers using a customer ID, name, and mobile number, with optional alternate mobile and personal or address details.

Service Management

Owner-maintained service information: name, department, category, application type, description, eligibility, required documents, process steps, expected processing time, status, and notes. The owner enters this information; nothing is retrieved automatically from government sources.

Applications

Applications connect a customer to a service. Several services in one visit are handled as separate applications under the same visit. Statuses: Received, In Progress, Waiting for Documents, Submitted, Completed, Rejected, Cancelled.

Queue / Token Management

One global queue managed by the owner and staff: Call Next, Recall, Skip, Cancel, Complete, and Hold when documents are missing. Customers do not book tokens online.

Collections

An owner-side record of the customer, application and service, amount, payment method, date and time, reference or receipt, and notes.

Staff / Permissions & Reports

The owner manages staff access. Collections, reports, settings, and staff management are owner-only areas. Reporting covers daily, weekly, and monthly operations.

Challenges

  • Designing a practical workflow for a physical service center
  • Separating customer-facing service information from internal financial information
  • Managing queue state and application state separately
  • Designing permissions for owner and staff
  • Keeping the system simple enough for daily use

What This Project Demonstrates

  • Translating a real-world process into a digital system
  • Building around an actual user's needs
