Bikergram Website

Research/ Development Path

Overview

Bikergram Website is a planned digital platform for organizing motorcycle-community and rider-focused information currently scattered across social media, maps, travel platforms, and local sources. It's designed around structured discovery of biker clubs, destinations, events, travel circuits and rider resources, with evidence-oriented research and data-quality practices for Andhra Pradesh and Telangana.

The Problem

Information that matters to bikers — clubs, destinations, events, travel circuits, local resources — exists, but it's spread thin across social media posts, map pins, travel sites, and word of mouth, with no shared source of truth. Bringing it together in one structured, verifiable place is the starting point for this project.

The Approach

The approach centers on structured discovery and evidence-oriented research: verifying information before it's treated as reliable, organizing it with a consistent data model, and building an information architecture that holds up as the dataset of clubs, places, events, and circuits grows.

Community research Product planning Data organization Verification methodology Information architecture

My Role

I'm researching and planning this platform myself — defining its information architecture, verification methodology, and data model, drawing on my experience running Bikergram's community. It's currently in the research and planning phase; development follows once that groundwork is in place.

Core Features

Bikers Hub is the discovery concept planned at the core of the website.

Club Discovery

Find Clubs → State → District → Details. Browse biker clubs narrowed down by state and district.

Places Discovery

Find Places → State → District → Details. Browse touring destinations narrowed down the same way.

Challenges

  • Verifying scattered, informally-shared information before treating it as reliable
  • Designing a data-quality and verification methodology that can hold up as the dataset grows
  • Turning community knowledge into structured, well-organized data
  • Planning an information architecture for clubs, places, events, and circuits that stays easy to navigate
  • Staying honest about what's research versus what's actually built

What This Project Demonstrates

  • Research methodology and evidence-oriented thinking
  • Product planning, grounded in a real community's needs
  • Information architecture and data organization
  • Translating informal community knowledge into a structured product
  • Working carefully in the research/planning phase before building further
